# Product vision

Relationship Intelligence is a **research-grade relationship system with a
real product implementation**. The research question is how personal and
professional relationship attention can be supported computationally
without corrupting the evidence it runs on: what was logged, what was
derived, and what the user asked for — kept strictly apart. The product —
a working system with UI and API — operationalizes that idea and tests it
against real contact history every day. That is why the product is
designed around provenance, determinism, confirmation gates, and honest
empty states: the research question demands them, and the product proves
they can be engineered.

It began with a simple question: **which of my relationships needs me
this week, and how do I know?** That leads past contact storage. A real
answer needs the history of contact, each relationship's own rhythm, the
things already promised, the dates approaching, and the user's own intent
— assembled into "here are five people and exactly why", not a score to
decode. The trajectory from here is to make that answer richer,
longitudinal, comparative, and interrogable — until the product preserves
not just who you know, but the living state of every relationship.

## The foundation already built

The working core, in production today: deterministic tie-decay scoring
with per-relationship rhythm measurement; an attention queue where every
row carries its reason; a weekly digest with signed one-click action
links; outreach batches whose drafts are grounded per person or flagged
thin; a meeting pipeline where planned context never becomes evidence;
AI-assisted ingestion where pasted text becomes reviewable proposals;
per-person cadence, reminders, and intentional-contact preferences that
constrain surfacing without touching scores; and an agentic Copilot over
~40 read tools plus confirmation-gated writes, answering "who works at
Proceedit?" with 15 cited names and "who did I call since last week?"
from the actual log.

## 1. From stored contacts to watched relationships

The attention queue is the foundation: not "we hold your contacts" but
"we watch how every relationship moves and tell you before drift becomes
loss." Increasing resolution is the natural next layer: richer rhythm
models, household/shared-relationship views, longer horizons, and
cross-device capture — so the answer stays correct as networks grow past
what memory can hold.

## 2. From queue rows to relationship lifecycles

The trajectory history already snapshots urgency over time. The larger
arc follows relationships through phases — warming, steady, drifting,
recovered — resolving phase identity from stored states only, under the
same no-fabrication discipline. The transition is **from ranking this
week to narrating the relationship's arc**, so a user can ask how things
evolved, not only who needs them now.

## 3. From one network to comparative networks

Recruiters and owners already filter one network by organization, role,
and contact evidence. The deeper question is comparative: how talent
pools, client portfolios, or community cohorts differ in attention load,
coverage, and risk — still descriptive, still per-relationship evidence,
extending the current filter contract to saved views and cohort
comparison.

## 4. From suggestions to structured provenance

Outputs already connect to underlying evidence: citations, evidence
drawers, source excerpts, audit rows. Formalizing that into a queryable
provenance model — observed vs derived vs user-confirmed vs suggested —
makes the system increasingly inspectable, letting power users filter by
evidence grade. Provenance becomes a property of the system, not of its
prose.

## 5. From one channel to an engagement ecosystem

Drafts already adapt per channel (email, LinkedIn, text, call prep) with
per-person overrides. The product idea is not channel accumulation —
different channels carry different relationship weight, and the system
should learn which channel actually sustains each relationship, with
per-channel evidence preserved rather than averaged away.

## 6. From reminders to attention cooperation

Reminders already exist as pure user intent, cooperating with — never
overwriting — derived urgency. The next layer studies the cooperation
itself: which reminders precede real contact, which get snoozed forever,
what cadence the user actually sustains vs what they set. Snooze/skip
patterns stay behavioral data about the user, never fabricated contact
about the relationship.

## 7. From product API to research platform

The system already produces scored states, snapshots, audit trails, and
evidence-graded memory. Exposing these as stable, versioned corpora turns
the product into infrastructure: researchers studying tie decay,
professional attention, and recruiter behavior get timestamped,
user-isolated material built for computational work — with construct
definitions precise enough to cite (see
[for-researchers](for-researchers.md)).

## 8. From Copilot to an interrogable network

The Copilot's bounded tools over already-scoped data point toward a
fuller interface: a user interrogating their whole network — which
narratives dominate, how two talent pools differ, what was promised to
whom and when. The AI's value is making the network interrogable while
evidence, provenance, and confirmation gates stay attached to every
answer. The constraint that gives this power is scope: the interface
never sees a broader context than the evidence the question is allowed
to see.

## Principles that carry forward

These constraints are what make the trajectory trustworthy, and they
travel with every extension: intention/state/interaction separation,
evidence-traceable answers, deterministic-where-appropriate scoring,
confirmation-gated writes, degradation-aware honesty (empty states over
invented data), and user isolation on every row.
